Most founders hire help later than they should. Early on, doing everything yourself feels efficient: you know how it's done and it's faster than explaining. Then the business grows, and the same habits quietly become the bottleneck.

A virtual assistant is often the first hire that gives a founder time back. Here's how to tell when it's time.

:::takeaways
- If recurring admin keeps pushing strategy, sales, or product work to "later", it's time.
- Onboarding takes hours once. The tasks then leave your plate every week.
- Start with frequent, rules-based, low-risk tasks that drain your energy.
- If your real need is protecting one leader's time, consider an executive assistant instead.
:::

## Seven signs you're ready

### 1. You're doing the same tasks every week

Scheduling, inbox cleanup, invoicing follow-ups, CRM updates. If a task repeats and follows a pattern, someone else can do it.

### 2. Important work keeps getting pushed to "later"

When strategy, sales, or product work only happens in the evenings, admin is crowding out the work only you can do.

### 3. Small things are slipping

Missed follow-ups, late replies, forgotten renewals. These rarely happen because you don't care. They happen because there's too much to track.

### 4. You're answering the same questions repeatedly

If customers, clients, or vendors ask the same things over and over, those answers can be documented and handled by someone else.

### 5. Your calendar controls your day

When scheduling back-and-forth and meeting logistics eat your mornings, it's time for help. We break down this specific problem in [inbox and calendar management](/solutions/inbox-calendar-management).

### 6. Growth is limited by your availability

If new clients, projects, or opportunities wait on your personal bandwidth, support is a growth decision, not just a convenience.

### 7. You feel like you can't take a day off

If the business stalls whenever you step away, too much depends on you alone.

:::checklist Quick self-check
- [x] I do the same admin tasks every week
- [x] Important work only happens after hours
- [x] Follow-ups are slipping
- [x] I answer the same questions repeatedly
- [x] My calendar decides my day
- [x] Growth waits on my availability
- [x] Things stall when I take a day off

If three or more sound familiar, support will likely pay for itself in time.
:::

## "But I don't have time to train someone"

This is the most common objection, and it's understandable. Handing work over takes effort up front.

The math usually works out, though. Onboarding a few recurring tasks might take several hours over the first weeks. After that, those tasks are off your plate every week, for good. The time you invest once comes back repeatedly.

The key is to start small. Hand over three to five recurring tasks, document them simply, and expand from there. Our guide on [onboarding a remote virtual assistant](/blog/how-to-onboard-a-remote-virtual-assistant) walks through the first 30 days.

## What to hand over first

Look for tasks that are:

- **Frequent**, happening daily or weekly
- **Rules-based**, following the same steps each time
- **Low-risk**, where a mistake is easy to catch and fix
- **Draining**, taking energy you'd rather spend elsewhere

Common first handoffs include inbox triage, scheduling, data entry, invoice follow-ups, and research.

## When you might not be ready yet

A virtual assistant isn't the right answer in every situation:

- **If you don't know what you'd hand over.** Spend a week tracking your time first.
- **If every task needs your judgment.** Start by documenting how you decide, so some of it can be delegated.
- **If the real problem is strategy, not capacity.** Support creates time. It doesn't decide what to do with it.
