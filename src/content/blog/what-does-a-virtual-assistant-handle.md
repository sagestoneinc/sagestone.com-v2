"Get a virtual assistant" is common advice for busy founders. What's less common is a clear answer to the next question: *what exactly would they do?* Without that answer, many teams hire support, hand over a few scattered tasks, and wonder why nothing feels lighter.

This guide breaks down the work a virtual assistant can reliably own, the work that should stay with you, and a simple way to decide what to hand over first.

:::takeaways
- A virtual assistant owns **recurring, rules-based work**: anything weekly, checklist-shaped, and not dependent on your judgment.
- Inbox, scheduling, admin, follow-through, and research are the usual starting points.
- Keep direction-setting decisions, key relationships, and pattern-less judgment calls.
- Start with 2–3 draining tasks and **write down how you do them** before handing over.
:::

## The short answer

A good virtual assistant handles **recurring, rules-based work that follows a pattern**. If a task happens every day or week, can be explained in a checklist, and doesn't require your personal judgment each time, it's a strong candidate.

That covers far more of a typical week than most people expect.

## Work a virtual assistant can own

### Inbox and calendar

This is where most engagements start, because it's where most time leaks.

- Triaging email, labeling, archiving, and surfacing what's urgent
- Drafting routine replies for your approval
- Scheduling, rescheduling, and protecting focus time
- Preparing agendas and briefing notes before important meetings

If this is your biggest pain point, see [inbox and calendar management](/solutions/inbox-calendar-management).

### Administrative work

- Data entry and record keeping across spreadsheets and tools
- Document formatting, proofreading, and file organization
- Vendor coordination and deadline tracking
- Recurring weekly and monthly tasks run from a checklist

### Customer and client follow-through

- Following up on quotes, proposals, and open questions
- Sending reminders and confirmations
- Keeping your CRM current so nothing slips (more on that in [CRM administration](/solutions/crm-administration))

### Research

- Comparing vendors, tools, or options and summarizing the tradeoffs
- Gathering background before calls or decisions
- Compiling lists, contacts, and structured information

## Work that should stay with you

Delegation works best when the boundaries are clear. Keep these with you or your leadership team:

- **Decisions that set direction.** Pricing, strategy, hiring, and anything that changes how the business works.
- **Relationships only you can hold.** Key clients, partners, and investors expect you.
- **Judgment calls without a pattern.** If you can't describe how you'd decide, it isn't ready to hand over yet.

A virtual assistant can still *support* this work by preparing information, drafting options, and handling the logistics, so your time goes to the decision itself.

## How to decide what to delegate first

Try this in one sitting:

1. **List everything you did last week**, including small tasks.
2. **Mark each task** as recurring or one-off, and rules-based or judgment-based.
3. **Circle the recurring, rules-based tasks.** That's your delegation list.
4. **Start with the two or three that drain the most time or energy**, not the easiest ones.
5. **Write down how you do them today**, even roughly. A short screen recording works too.

That last step matters more than any other. The quality of support depends on the clarity of the handoff. If your processes live only in your head, start by [documenting them](/blog/how-to-document-repetitive-processes).

:::tip
If a task only lives in your head, record yourself doing it once. A ten-minute screen recording is often enough for someone else to take it over.
:::

## Virtual assistant or executive assistant?

The difference is less about tasks and more about scope and trust.

- A **virtual assistant** typically owns defined, recurring work across admin, inbox, scheduling, and follow-up.
- An **executive assistant** works more closely with one leader, managing priorities, communication, and time with more discretion and context.

## Making it work

The businesses that get the most from a virtual assistant do three things consistently:

- **They start with a clear list**, not a vague "help me with everything."
- **They document the work**, so quality doesn't depend on memory.
- **They review early and often**, especially in the first few weeks.
