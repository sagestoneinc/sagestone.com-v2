Founders are usually the last people to notice they've become the bottleneck. The business grows, and every approval, update, and follow-up still runs through them.

Delegation is the fix, but "delegate more" is vague advice. Here are twelve specific operational tasks that founders commonly hold onto, and that someone else can usually run well with a little documentation.

## Communication and coordination

### 1. Inbox triage

Sorting, labeling, archiving, and flagging what actually needs you. Most founders only need to see a fraction of their email.

### 2. Scheduling

Booking meetings, handling reschedules, and protecting focus time. The back-and-forth adds up to hours every week. See [inbox and calendar management](/solutions/inbox-calendar-management).

### 3. Meeting follow-ups

Sending recap notes, tracking action items, and nudging people on deadlines.

## Customers and clients

### 4. Routine customer questions

Order status, account access, how-to questions. Document the answers once and let someone else handle them.

### 5. Client onboarding steps

Welcome emails, kickoff scheduling, and collecting information. A checklist makes this repeatable. See [customer onboarding support](/solutions/customer-onboarding-support).

### 6. CRM updates

Logging calls, updating deal stages, and cleaning up contacts. Accurate data shouldn't depend on the founder's memory. See [CRM administration](/solutions/crm-administration).

## Finance and admin

### 7. Invoice preparation and follow-up

Preparing invoices for approval and sending polite payment reminders. Approvals stay with you. The chasing doesn't have to.

### 8. Expense and receipt tracking

Collecting receipts, categorizing expenses, and keeping records ready for your bookkeeper.

### 9. Vendor coordination

Requesting quotes, tracking renewals, and handling routine vendor questions.

## Systems and documentation

### 10. Writing SOPs

Turning how things are done into clear procedures. This is the task that makes every other handoff easier. See [how to document repetitive processes](/blog/how-to-document-repetitive-processes).

### 11. Weekly reporting

Pulling numbers from your tools into a simple weekly summary, so you review insights instead of building spreadsheets.

### 12. Tool and file hygiene

Organizing shared drives, archiving old projects, and keeping your project tool current.

## How to choose what to hand off first

Score each task from 1 to 3 on two questions:

1. **How much time does it take each week?**
2. **How much does it drain your energy?**

Start with the tasks that score highest on both. Then check one more thing: **can the task be described in steps?** If yes, it's ready to delegate. If not, document your decision process first.

## What should stay with you

Delegation works best with clear boundaries. Keep these close:

- Strategy, pricing, and major financial decisions
- Key relationships with clients, partners, and investors
- Hiring decisions and team leadership
- Judgment calls that don't follow a pattern yet

Your support can still prepare information and handle logistics around these, so your time goes to the decision itself.

## Start small, then build

You don't need to hand over all twelve at once. Pick three, document them, and hand them over properly. Once those run smoothly, add the next few. Within a couple of months, your week looks very different.
